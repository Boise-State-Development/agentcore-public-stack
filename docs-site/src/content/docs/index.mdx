{/* ── Why this exists ───────────────────────────────────────────────── */}

<section id="why" class="lp-section lp-reveal">
	<div class="lp-manifesto">
		<p class="lp-eyebrow"><span class="lp-eyebrow-num">✱</span> why this exists</p>
		<h2 class="lp-h2">public institutions share <span class="lp-accent">one AI dilemma.</span></h2>
		<p class="lp-lede">
			Per-seat subscriptions do not scale to a campus or a workforce. Student, employee and
			resident data has to stay under institutional control. And the vendor landscape changes
			every quarter, while each vendor pitches its own ecosystem as the safe place to wait it
			out. At 30,000 users, commercial subscriptions would cost upwards of $7.2M a year. This
			platform delivers the same capabilities for the cost of the AWS resources people
			actually use.
		</p>
		<blockquote class="lp-quote">
			<p>"Keep your harness separate from the model."</p>
			<cite>— Satya Nadella, Microsoft CEO, on making any model swappable · <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/29/microsoft-is-openly-competing-with-openai-anthropic-more-than-ever/">TechCrunch, July 2026</a></cite>
		</blockquote>
		<div class="lp-manifesto-grid">
			<div class="lp-manifesto-item">
				<h3>The harness is yours.</h3>
				<p>The chat surface, agents, tools and governance are open-source code deployed in your AWS account: infrastructure you control, not a subscription you rent. The only harness truly separate from the model is one you can read, fork and run yourself.</p>
			</div>
			<div class="lp-manifesto-item">
				<h3>Models are swappable.</h3>
				<p>Claude, Nova, Llama, Mistral, GPT, Gemini and more, curated by your admins and governed by role. Switch models in the middle of a conversation. When a better or cheaper model ships, you add it to the catalog that afternoon, with no redeploy.</p>
			</div>
			<div class="lp-manifesto-item">
				<h3>Your data stays put.</h3>
				<p>Conversations, documents and knowledge bases live inside a security boundary you already operate. Models are used for inference only and never train on your data. Leaving costs nothing: no proprietary formats, no per-seat contract to unwind.</p>
			</div>
		</div>
	</div>
</section>

{/* ── Who it's for ──────────────────────────────────────────────────── */}

<section id="public-sector" class="lp-section lp-reveal">
	<p class="lp-eyebrow"><span class="lp-eyebrow-num">⌂</span> built for the public sector</p>
	<h2 class="lp-h2">shaped by a university, <span class="lp-accent">ready for an agency.</span></h2>
	<p class="lp-lede">
		AgentCore Public Stack is the codebase behind the AI platform that serves students,
		faculty and staff at Boise State University. It is general-purpose, but every design
		decision was made for an institution that answers to the public.
	</p>
	<div class="lp-manifesto-grid">
		<div class="lp-manifesto-item">
			<h3>For universities</h3>
			<p>Faculty build a course assistant from instructions, course materials and the tools it needs, then share it with a class: every student gets the same assistant under the same guardrails. Research offices and labs work in Shared Projects. Canvas, Google Drive and campus systems become tools, with consent asked for in the conversation.</p>
		</div>
		<div class="lp-manifesto-item">
			<h3>For state and local government</h3>
			<p>Ground staff and constituent assistants in your own ordinances, policy manuals and benefits guides, re-synced as they change. Upload documents up to 50 MB, whole Drive folders and Word files with tracked changes, and get Word, Excel and PowerPoint back. Sign in through the identity provider you already run.</p>
		</div>
		<div class="lp-manifesto-item">
			<h3>Accountable by design</h3>
			<p>Roles mapped from identity-provider claims. Admin surfaces delegated to the office that owns them. An audit log of every admin change. Quotas in dollars, per role or per person, and every model call metered to the user, session, agent and model that made it. Equitable access: premium capability is a role grant, not a price tier.</p>
		</div>
	</div>
</section>

<section id="chat" class="lp-section lp-reveal">
	<p class="lp-eyebrow"><span class="lp-eyebrow-num">01</span> the conversation</p>
	<h2 class="lp-h2">chat that ships <span class="lp-accent">finished.</span></h2>
	<p class="lp-lede">
		Not a demo loop: the whole conversational surface. Token-by-token streaming, artifacts in a
		docked panel, voice in and out, and conversations that manage their own context instead
		of falling over.
	</p>
	<div class="lp-feature">
		<ul class="lp-points">
			<li><strong>Streaming, end to end.</strong> Tool calls, interactive tool UIs, status and errors all arrive on one stream. The UI says what the agent is doing while it does it.</li>
			<li><strong>Artifacts.</strong> Documents, charts, pages and Markdown with math open in a side panel with versioned history and safe sharing.</li>
			<li><strong>Voice and dictation.</strong> Talk with the assistant through Amazon Nova Sonic, or dictate into the composer.</li>
			<li><strong>Durable conversations.</strong> Pin, search every conversation with <code>Cmd/Ctrl+K</code>, steer a running turn, and let the agent ask a structured clarifying question when it needs one. Long conversations compact themselves, visibly.</li>
			<li><strong>Or stay in your terminal.</strong> <code>aps-tui</code> is the same assistant in your shell. With your approval on each call, it can read, write and run things on your machine.</li>
		</ul>
		<Screenshot

<section id="agents" class="lp-section lp-reveal">
	<p class="lp-eyebrow"><span class="lp-eyebrow-num">02</span> agents &amp; skills</p>
	<h2 class="lp-h2">build an agent once. <span class="lp-accent">your whole org runs it.</span></h2>
	<p class="lp-lede">
		Agents bundle instructions, a model, tools, skills and a knowledge base into something
		anyone can use. The Agent Designer composes them from what your role allows, and the
		marketplace puts them in front of the people who need them.
	</p>
	<div class="lp-feature lp-feature--flip">
		<Screenshot
			src={agentDesigner}
			alt="The Agent Designer editing a demo introductory biology tutor: persona, instructions and model on the left, and a live preview on the right answering a question about mitosis and meiosis."
			title="Agent Designer · live preview"
		/>
		<ul class="lp-points">
			<li><strong>Agent Designer.</strong> Persona, model, tools, skills and knowledge in one form, with a live preview that runs the saved agent.</li>
			<li><strong>Marketplace.</strong> Publish with reach controls and admin review. Teammates pin, duplicate and report from the listing.</li>
			<li><strong>Version snapshots.</strong> Every publish is a snapshot; roll back a bad release without touching the editor.</li>
			<li><strong>@-mentions and sharing.</strong> Pull another agent into a conversation. Share with viewers and editors: ownership is an access gate, not a silo.</li>
			<li><strong>Skills.</strong> Portable, versioned instruction bundles with reference files and bound tools, triggered with <code>/</code>. The agent can write and refine one, with your approval on every write.</li>
		</ul>
	</div>
</section>

<section id="projects" class="lp-section lp-reveal">
	<p class="lp-eyebrow"><span class="lp-eyebrow-num">03</span> shared projects</p>
	<h2 class="lp-h2">one workspace, <span class="lp-accent">one assistant, the whole team.</span></h2>
	<p class="lp-lede">
		A project is a shared workspace for an office, a lab or a committee: owner, editor and
		viewer roles, and one assistant everyone works with. Its instructions, model, tools, skills
		and files belong to the project, so a change one editor makes applies to every member's
		next turn.
	</p>
	<div class="lp-feature">
		<ul class="lp-points">

			<li><strong>Project memory.</strong> Governed shared memory, with each member's own memory beside it.</li>
			<li><strong>Private until shared.</strong> Conversations stay private to their author until they share one with the project.</li>
			<li><strong>Schedules, activity and history.</strong> Put a prompt on a schedule that delivers to the project, and see who changed what.</li>
		</ul>

	<h2 class="lp-h2">answers grounded in <span class="lp-accent">your documents.</span></h2>
	<p class="lp-lede">
		Agents and projects carry their own knowledge bases on Amazon Bedrock, inside your account.
		Conversations take large documents, whole folders and Word files with their tracked changes,
		and the agent produces Office documents in return.
	</p>
	<div class="lp-feature lp-feature--flip">
		<Screenshot
			src={projectFiles}
			alt="A project's Files page listing four demo grant-office documents with who added each one, three marked Ready and one still Indexing, and buttons to add files, web content or a Drive connection."
			title="Project files"
		/>
		<ul class="lp-points">
			<li><strong>Per-agent and per-project knowledge bases.</strong> Each retrieves from its own corpus, with optional source citations.</li>
			<li><strong>Kept in sync.</strong> Sync policies re-index connected sources on a schedule, so answers track the documents as they change.</li>
			<li><strong>Large documents and folders.</strong> Files up to 50 MB are prepared once and read by reference; a Drive folder arrives as a manifest the agent navigates.</li>
			<li><strong>Word tracked changes.</strong> Insertions and deletions reach the model as written, and the agent can redline a document back.</li>
			<li><strong>Office output.</strong> Word, Excel and PowerPoint, charts and diagrams, produced as artifacts.</li>
		</ul>
	</div>
</section>

{/* ── 05 · Tools & MCP ──────────────────────────────────────────────── */}

<section id="tools" class="lp-section lp-reveal">
	<p class="lp-eyebrow"><span class="lp-eyebrow-num">05</span> tools &amp; integrations</p>
	<h2 class="lp-h2">connect the systems <span class="lp-accent">you already run.</span></h2>
	<p class="lp-lede">
		Direct tools, AWS SDK calls, MCP servers behind AgentCore Gateway, external MCP servers and
		remote A2A agents, in one governed catalog. Register an MCP server in the admin console and
		its tools are discovered, assigned to roles and available, with no redeploy.
	</p>
	<div class="lp-feature">
		<ul class="lp-points">
			<li><strong>MCP Apps.</strong> A tool can render its own interactive UI inline, animating as the model writes its arguments.</li>
			<li><strong>OAuth in the conversation.</strong> A tool that needs the user's consent asks for it mid-turn and resumes after sign-in.</li>
			<li><strong>Per-tool enablement.</strong> Expose exactly the tools each role needs, down to single tools within a server.</li>
			<li><strong>Browser hand-off.</strong> The agent browses the web, and when a site needs the user to sign in, hands them the browser in a docked live view.</li>
			<li><strong>Your systems.</strong> Canvas, Google Workspace, PeopleSoft, GitHub, library and records systems, or any internal API wrapped in an MCP server.</li>
		</ul>

{/* ── 06 · Governance ───────────────────────────────────────────────── */}

<section id="governance" class="lp-section lp-reveal">
	<p class="lp-eyebrow"><span class="lp-eyebrow-num">06</span> governance, quotas &amp; cost</p>
	<h2 class="lp-h2">budgets everyone can see, <span class="lp-accent">down to the token.</span></h2>
	<p class="lp-lede">
		Consumption billing only works for an institution if spend is predictable per person and
		visible before it becomes a problem. Quotas are enforced in dollars on every surface (web,
		terminal and API keys), and every model call is metered, priced and attributable.
	</p>
	<div class="lp-feature lp-feature--flip">
		<Screenshot
			src={adminCosts}
			alt="The admin Cost Analytics page on the built-in Demo Month sample data: total cost, average cost per user, active users and cache savings, with a donut chart of spend by model."
			title="Admin · cost analytics"
		/>
		<ul class="lp-points">
			<li><strong>Quota tiers.</strong> A monthly limit and an optional daily one, attached to a role, an identity-provider claim, an email domain or a person. Each tier blocks or warns at the limit.</li>
			<li><strong>Warnings before the wall.</strong> Warnings in the conversation as the limit nears, plus a notice when one conversation alone has spent a large share of the month. A blocked turn is a message that says when the quota resets.</li>
			<li><strong>Overrides and an inspector.</strong> Timed overrides with a reason, a per-user inspector that explains which tier applied and why, and an exportable log of every enforcement event.</li>
			<li><strong>Cost analytics.</strong> Spend by user, model and period, with per-session and per-call drill-down and prompt-cache status on every call.</li>
			<li><strong>RBAC, delegated admin and audit.</strong> Roles from your IdP with inheritance, direct user grants, admin scopes per surface, and an audit log of who changed what.</li>
		</ul>
	</div>
</section>

{/* ── 07 · Deploy ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── */}

<section id="deploy" class="lp-section lp-reveal">
	<p class="lp-eyebrow"><span class="lp-eyebrow-num">07</span> your infrastructure</p>
	<h2 class="lp-h2">one stack. <span class="lp-accent">your AWS account.</span></h2>
	<p class="lp-lede">
		Everything deploys as a single CDK stack plus GitHub Actions workflows. No SaaS control
		plane, no per-seat licensing, no new contract: it runs on the AWS account and agreement
		you already have.
	</p>
	<div class="lp-feature">
		<div class="lp-terminal" role="img" aria-label="Deploy pipeline: one CDK stack, then backend images, then the frontend">
			<div class="lp-terminal-bar"><span class="shot-dots" aria-hidden="true"><i></i><i></i><i></i></span>deploy — bash</div>
			<pre class="lp-terminal-body">{`$ npx cdk deploy your-prefix-PlatformStack
✓ PlatformStack        one stack, every AWS resource

$ gh workflow run backend.yml
✓ app-api              ECS behind CloudFront
✓ inference-api        AgentCore Runtime container
✓ rag-ingestion        knowledge base pipeline
✓ artifact-render      artifact rendering

$ gh workflow run frontend-deploy.yml
✓ SPA → S3 + CloudFront

Deployed. Your models, your data, your bill.`}</pre>
		</div>
		<ul class="lp-points">
			<li><strong>Single CDK stack.</strong> Every resource lives in one <code>PlatformStack</code>, with no cross-stack ordering to babysit.</li>
			<li><strong>Code ships out of band.</strong> Images go through ECR to rolling service updates, so a code change deploys in minutes without touching infrastructure.</li>
			<li><strong>Off by default.</strong> New features ship behind flags and are turned on per environment, so a deployment never inherits half-finished work by upgrading.</li>
			<li><strong>SSO you already run.</strong> Entra ID, Cognito, Google or any OIDC issuer, with roles mapped from its claims.</li>
		</ul>
	</div>
</section>

{/* ── FAQ ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── */}

<section class="lp-section lp-section--narrow lp-reveal">
	<h2 class="lp-h2">frequently asked <span class="lp-accent">questions.</span></h2>
	<div class="lp-faq">
		<details>
			<summary>What is the AgentCore Public Stack?</summary>
			<p>
				A production-ready AI platform built on AWS Bedrock AgentCore and Strands Agents: an
				Angular web app, FastAPI backends, a terminal client and a CDK-defined AWS platform,
				developed in the open by Boise State University. It is the codebase that serves the
				university's own students, faculty and staff.
			</p>
		</details>
		<details>
			<summary>Who is it for?</summary>
			<p>
				Universities, colleges, and state and local government agencies that want to give
				everyone in the organization the same capable AI tools, under their own identity
				provider, policies and budget. Nothing in it is specific to Boise State: branding,
				models, tools and access are all configured in the admin console.
			</p>
		</details>
		<details>
			<summary>Can a government agency use it under the license?</summary>
			<p>
				The code is licensed under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0, which permits
				noncommercial use, including by universities, colleges, government agencies and other
				noncommercial institutions. Use in a product or service that generates revenue needs a
				separate commercial license; the
				<a href="https://github.com/Boise-State-Development/agentcore-public-stack/blob/main/LICENSE">LICENSE</a>
				file has the full text.
			</p>
		</details>
		<details>
			<summary>Is this an official AWS product?</summary>
			<p>
				No. It is the <em>unofficial</em> frontend for Strands and AgentCore: an independent
				open-source project that builds on those AWS services and tracks them closely.
			</p>
		</details>
		<details>
			<summary>Why not just buy Copilot or ChatGPT Enterprise?</summary>
			<p>
				Those are excellent products, rented from vendors who are increasingly competing with
				each other and repricing as they go. Renting the harness means renting the switching
				costs, and per-seat pricing grows with every person you add. Owning it means model
				choice, pricing, data handling and roadmap stay decisions you make, not terms you accept.
			</p>
		</details>
		<details>
			<summary>Which models does it support?</summary>
			<p>
				Admins curate a catalog from Amazon Bedrock (Claude, Nova, Llama, Mistral and more),
				OpenAI and Google Gemini, set pricing and defaults, and retire a model to a successor
				when it ages out. Users switch per conversation, and role-based access decides who can
				use which model. The model layer is Strands Agents, so any
				<a href="https://strandsagents.com/docs/user-guide/concepts/model-providers/">Strands
				model provider</a> can be added.
			</p>
		</details>
		<details>
			<summary>Where does my data live?</summary>
			<p>
				In your AWS account. Conversations, documents and knowledge bases stay inside your own
				security boundary, and models are used for inference only: your data is never used to
				train third-party models.
			</p>
		</details>
		<details>
			<summary>What does it cost to run, and how do we keep spend predictable?</summary>
			<p>
				There is no license fee: you pay AWS for what people use. Quota tiers cap spend in
				dollars per role, group or person, warn in the conversation before a limit, and either
				block or warn at it. Cost analytics and prompt-cache observability show where every
				dollar went, per user and per model call.
			</p>
		</details>
		<details>
			<summary>How do we roll it out without inheriting unfinished work?</summary>
			<p>
				New features ship off by default behind <a href="/agentcore-public-stack/configuration/feature-flags/">feature
				flags</a>, and a deployment turns each one on by choice. Upgrading never switches on
				something half-built.
			</p>
		</details>
		<details>
			<summary>How do I deploy it?</summary>
			<p>
				One CDK stack for infrastructure and GitHub Actions workflows for the application. The
				<a href="/agentcore-public-stack/deployment/overview/">deployment guide</a> walks
				through it end to end.
			</p>
		</details>
	</div>
</section>
