[S1] Every city has a sound, and most of the people who live there have stopped hearing it. It is the low hum of traffic on a distant road, the click of a crossing signal, the hiss of a bus kneeling at the curb. We notice it only when it stops.
[S1] In the spring of a quiet year, many cities did stop. Streets that had been loud for a century went still. And people who had lived their whole lives in those places said the same thing, in many different languages. They could hear birds.
[S1] The birds had always been there. What changed was the space around their songs. In a noisy street, a bird has to work harder to be heard. Researchers have found that city birds often sing louder than their country cousins, and some of them sing at a higher pitch, lifting their songs above the rumble of engines the way a person raises their voice in a crowded room.
[S1] Some birds even change when they sing. In parts of Europe, robins living near busy roads have been recorded singing at night, when the traffic finally thins and the air belongs to them again.
[S1] This is a small example of something much larger. The places we build do not only shape our own lives. They shape the lives of everything that shares them with us, often in ways we never intended and rarely notice.
[S1] A bright window confuses a moth. A glass tower confuses a migrating warbler. A paved lot changes how rain moves through the soil, and that changes which plants can grow, and that changes which insects arrive, and that changes which birds can raise their young.
[S1] None of this is a reason for despair. It is a reason for attention. When we learn to hear the city as the animals hear it, we can start to make small choices that help. Darker nights during migration. Trees along the streets. A patch of wild ground left alone at the edge of a park.
[S1] The quiet spring did not last. The traffic came back, as it was always going to. But for a few weeks, millions of people were reminded that they live in a shared place, full of other voices, most of which were never trying to compete with us at all.
[S1] Tomorrow morning, before the day gets loud, open a window and listen. You might be surprised by who has been singing all along.
