### 2. Bound the `document_read` pattern regex — measured exponential, model-supplied, hangs the turn
- **Source**: #1147's own residue entry, which ranks it **first** among the four unfixed findings; re-verified on disk today at `apis/shared/files/document_read.py:528`
- **Surface area**: backend — `document_read.py` `_compile()` / `_grep_lines()` and the three `_*_pattern` callers
- **Change**: bound the input (pattern length, line length, match budget) and/or run the scan under a hard timeout, instead of handing a raw model-supplied string to `re.compile`.
- **Subtracts**: yes — an unbounded input path. `_compile` correctly degrades an *invalid* regex to a literal via `re.escape`, which catches malformed patterns but not expensive ones; there is no length cap, no complexity check, and Python's `re` has **no match timeout**.
- **Effort**: Low
- **Impact**: Med–High — measured at **0.87 s at 24 characters, 3.5 s at 26, 14 s at 28**, exponential, against real PDF lines of **60–100**. `pattern` is model-supplied and the search runs in `asyncio.to_thread`, so **the turn hangs to the 600 s SSE timeout** — a user-visible dead conversation, not just a busy worker.
- **POC findings**: timings measured in the sweep. Not reproduced as a fleet-level availability event; the per-turn hang is the established consequence.
- **Ship means**: a PR bounding `_compile`/`_grep_lines`, with the exponential case as a test.
- **Decline means**: accept a model-reachable 600-second hang, and record it.
- **Recommendation**: **Ship.** The only item here with a measured exponential and no owner, and it is a small local fix in one function. Both #1147's author and this review independently ranked it top of the residue.

## Take

**The forum's shipping model works, and last week's Take was wrong about it.** Six of nine proposals converted in seven days — including the three standalone "ten-minute" comment fixes that last week argued could only land by riding a carrier. That thesis was a fair reading of a two-week sample and it did not survive the third week. What *did* survive is narrower and more specific: **engineering items convert reliably; items requiring an edit to `CLAUDE.md` or a skill file have never converted once.** That is not priority, it is authorship, and it is why Proposal #4's honest answer is now the Decline branch the forum pre-committed to.

**The debt this week left is a quality gate, not a velocity problem.** Two specs, written weeks apart, each chose the strongest possible gate wording — a veto — and each shipped with defaults live and the harness unbuilt. Neither is carelessness; a harness is the one deliverable in a cost epic that ships no user-visible behaviour, so it has no natural owner. The asymmetry is what matters: `CLAUDE.md` says quality wins when it conflicts with cost, and that is unfalsifiable while every cost change is measured and no quality change is. The good news is that this week also shipped the cheap version of the instrument — `F#` down-thumb rows split by turn class is a query, not a harness.

**If Phil ships three: #1** (merge #1147 — it is a review and a merge button, it fixes a 14×-wrong instrument and a kill switch that is worse than useless, and it lands the epic's own measurement plan), **#2** (the ReDoS — measured exponential, model-reachable, hangs the turn to the 600 s timeout, small local fix, and independently ranked first by the person who found it), and **#3's cheap half** (the turn-class down-thumb comparison, which closes the veto gap with a query rather than a build). **#4** needs one keystroke or one line of honesty, and after five cycles the line of honesty is worth more.

**A correction this document owes #1147, stated plainly because it changed the recommendation.** The first draft ranked "an experiment's default must be its control" as Proposal #3 and told Phil to move `DOCUMENT_OFFLOAD_ROLLOUT_PERCENT` off its default of 100. **That was wrong**, and the refutation was already sitting in #1147's own queue write, on a branch this review had not yet read: `offload_enabled_for` gates only two call sites, and **PR-3 — the digest-on-restore that is the epic's actual behaviour change — is not bucketed at all**, nor are PR-1, PR-2 or PR-6. The percent splits only the least consequential half and was never a control arm for PRs 1–3, which can only be measured before/after a deploy whatever it says. #1147's call — ship at 100%, count `document_offload` events instead, since PR-4 is predicted to fire ≈0 times because restore reaches the document first — is better reasoned and is adopted here. What survived is the narrower and truer finding: **the vetoes are unrun**, which both documents agree on. The process lesson is the one already in the Silence section: **the best analysis of the week arrived as a queue write on an unmerged branch, and a review that reads only `develop` will keep being corrected by it.**
