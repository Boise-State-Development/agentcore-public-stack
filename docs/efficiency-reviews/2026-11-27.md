### What moved week over week

- Cost per turn rose 94% ($0.049 → $0.0952) and turns fell 84% (18,596 → 3,014). Most of it is mix: the Agent that served 9,053 single-call turns the week before served almost none, and it was a holiday week (291 users against 1,225). Without each week's highest-volume Agent, turns fell 69% (9,343 → 2,890) and cost per turn rose 17% ($0.085 → $0.099).
- That 17% is not a model shift: cost per turn rose on every model (Sonnet 5 $0.068 → $0.096, Sonnet 5.5 $0.061 → $0.084, Opus 5.5 $0.266 → $0.290). The turns were heavier: 25% opened on an expired cache against 15%, 23% used a tool against 18%, and model calls per turn went from 1.46 to 1.66.
- Median turn time rose 16% (10.3 s → 11.9 s); without the top Agent, 10.3 s → 12.0 s and p90 44.5 s → 51.1 s, in step with the heavier turns.
- Median first token improved 15% overall (2.0 s → 1.7 s), but that is the top Agent leaving the mix: without it the median went 1.5 s → 1.7 s and p90 8.8 s → 11.1 s. Opus 5.5 moved most (1.9 s → 3.0 s median, 276 turns). More returns after an idle gap explain part of it (candidate 3); the rest is a lead to re-read next week on normal volume.
- Share of tool turns with an error fell 22% (12.1% → 9.5%), in step with the `knowledge_files`, `analyze_spreadsheet` and `fetch_url_content` fixes.
- Model mix: Sonnet 5.5 went from 23% to 40% of weekly spend, Sonnet 5 from 49% to 22%, Opus 5.5 from 24% to 33%.
- By release, 1.38.0 to 1.40.0 read $0.09–0.12 a turn against $0.04–0.07 before. They ran only in the quiet week without the high-volume Agent, so this is the same mix shift, not a release regression; the release readout is the reader for that question.
- 28-day totals: spend $3,449 → $3,072 (−11%), calls 79,175 → 65,036, users 1,918 → 1,836.
